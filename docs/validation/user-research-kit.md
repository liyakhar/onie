# Wollie user-research kit

Use this kit without explaining Wollie's intended model. The goal is to discover whether people understand it naturally, not to teach them how it works.

## Participant mix

Recruit 10 couples who live together and manage at least one recurring household expense.

- 3 couples using mostly joint accounts.
- 3 couples using mostly separate accounts.
- 4 couples using both joint and separate accounts.
- Include unequal incomes, at least two unmarried couples, and at least two couples saving for a shared goal.
- Do not recruit only friends who already understand Wollie.

## Recruitment message

> We are studying how couples manage everyday spending, bills and savings. This is a 30-minute research conversation, not a sales call. We are interested in couples using joint accounts, separate accounts, or both. We will not ask for bank credentials or exact account balances.

## Screener

1. Do you currently live with a partner?
2. Do you share responsibility for rent, bills, groceries, savings, or another recurring cost?
3. Do you use joint accounts, separate accounts, or both?
4. Who usually manages or checks the household finances?
5. Have you used a budgeting app, spreadsheet, Splitwise, or a banking analytics tool in the last year?

Exclude participants who work on Wollie or have already received a detailed product explanation.

## Round 1: problem interview

Do not show the product.

1. Walk me through the last month of managing money together.
2. What happened when each income arrived?
3. How did you decide who paid for rent, groceries and other shared costs?
4. Tell me about the last time the system became confusing or unfair.
5. How do you know what is safe to spend before the next income?
6. How do you track savings, tax, pension or future purchases?
7. Which information do you want your partner to see? Is anything personal?
8. What have you tried before? Why did you stop or continue?
9. What happens when one person earns more?
10. If you could remove one part of this work, what would it be?

Avoid “Would you use Wollie?” and other hypothetical questions.

## Round 2: unassisted comprehension test

Show the current product with realistic demo data. Ask the participant to think aloud.

Tasks:

1. Tell me how much this household can spend on everyday purchases right now.
2. Tell me how much was spent on food and what remains.
3. Tell me how much is planned for pension and how much actually reached pension.
4. Find the next expected household payment.
5. Show which account belongs to which person.
6. Explain what would happen if another €2,000 income arrived.
7. Categorize a grocery transaction incorrectly marked as Shopping.
8. Invite your partner or explain what you expect the invitation to do.

Do not rescue a participant until they have stopped making progress for 30 seconds.

## Post-test questions

1. What do you think Wollie does?
2. Which number would you check most often?
3. Which number did you trust least, and why?
4. What did “reserved,” “available,” and “contributed” mean to you?
5. Would Wollie replace anything you currently use?
6. What would stop you from connecting your real accounts?
7. Would your partner use this too, or would one person manage it?
8. What would you expect to pay, if anything?

## Decision thresholds

The product direction reaches the 92 pre-build threshold only when:

- 7 of 10 couples describe the core coordination problem without prompting.
- Average problem severity is at least 7/10.
- 80% of test participants answer four of the five core comprehension questions correctly.
- 4 of 5 couples complete the core workflow without help.
- Every financial test case reconciles to its source transactions.

If any threshold fails, record the failure before proposing a change. Do not add features to compensate for unclear fundamentals.
